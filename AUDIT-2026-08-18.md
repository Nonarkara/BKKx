## 1. The Health Index contradicts itself on screen — **the one real defect**

`/shophouses/global` renders this, server-side (confirmed in the built worker
output, 200, 278,077 bytes):

> **Where Singapore is.** Composite **1.4** — **the floor of the index on
> purpose.** The HDB-style shophouse revival with a lifestyle economy on top
> of it is what the Bangkok Sukhumvit 71 corridor should not end up as.

`<RankingBoard />` renders immediately below that paragraph. Recomputing the
composite for all 19 towns straight from `shophouse-global.ts`
(`compositeScore` = simple mean of the five 1–5 metrics):

| # | Town | Composite | a c v r w | verdict |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | hongjiang | 4.8 | 5 5 4 5 5 | ideal |
| 2 | vigan | 4.6 | 5 5 4 4 5 | vulnerable |
| 3 | kuching | 4.0 | 4 4 4 4 4 | thriving |
| … | … | … | … | … |
| 9 | **bangkok-sukhumvit-71** | 3.0 | 3 3 4 2 3 | stable |
| … | … | … | … | … |
| 16 | lijiang | **1.4** | 2 1 2 1 1 | lost |
| 17 | **singapore-chinatown** | **1.4** | 2 1 2 1 1 | lost |
| 18 | zhouzhuang | **1.4** | 2 1 2 1 1 | lost |
| 19 | **shanghai-shikumen** | **1.2** | 2 1 1 1 1 | lost |

Singapore is **not** the floor. Shanghai shikumen sits strictly below it at
1.2, and Lijiang and Zhouzhuang are tied with it at 1.4. The page asserts a
superlative that the table directly beneath it disproves — a reader scrolling
one screen sees the contradiction.

This matters more than a normal copy error because the whole page is built on
the claim that its numbers are computed rather than asserted. The number
(`1.4`) is computed correctly; the **word** "floor" around it is hand-written
and was never re-checked after the other towns were scored.

Three honest fixes, cheapest first:

1. Re-word to what is true and still makes the argument: *"Composite 1.4 —
   the bottom of the index, tied with Lijiang and Zhouzhuang."*
2. Say what was probably meant: Singapore is the lowest-scoring town that is
   still a **working commercial district** (Shanghai shikumen is largely
   residential/redeveloped). If that is the intended claim, state that
   qualifier — it is a stronger point than the current one.
3. Derive the word instead of typing it: compute `RANKED_TOWNS.at(-1)` and
   render "the floor" only when Singapore actually is it.

Option 3 is the one consistent with the rest of the file's discipline.

## 2. Melaka and George Town publish identical UNESCO areas

Both towns render the same figures on their own cards
(`global/page.tsx:360` prints `{t.unesco.areaHa} ha + {t.unesco.bufferHa} ha buffer`):

| Town | whcId | inscribed | criteria | areaHa | bufferHa |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| melaka | 1223 | 2008 | ii, iii, iv | **109.68** | **376.27** |
| george-town | 1223 | 2008 | ii, iii, iv | **109.68** | **376.27** |

They are one joint inscription (WHC 1223) but two physically different
component towns of different sizes. The same pair of numbers cannot be each
town's own core and buffer zone. Either these are the **whole-property
totals** — in which case they are correct but mislabelled, and the card should
say "joint property, both components" — or they are one component's figures
copied onto the other, in which case one card is wrong.

The other six UNESCO blocks (vigan 502, hoi-an 948, pingyao 812, lijiang 811,
galle 451, luang-prabang 479) each carry distinct figures, so this looks like
a copy-paste at the time the two Malaysian entries were written.

Reference numbers, inscription years and criteria all look right across all
eight. **I could not verify the areas against UNESCO — `whc.unesco.org` is
blocked by this environment's egress proxy** (see §6). The finding above is
from internal impossibility, not from checking the source.

## 3. Telemetry is per-event, but the rule says aggregate

`site/CLAUDE.md` states: *"D1 stores aggregate pageviews only; never collect
IP addresses or credentials."*

The explicit prohibitions are honoured — no `cf-connecting-ip`, no
`x-forwarded-for`, no credential capture anywhere in `site/worker/` or
`site/db/`. But the table is not aggregate:

```
pageviews(id, path, referrer, country, language, user_agent, created_at)
```

`recordPageview` writes **one row per request** with the user-agent string
(truncated to 512 chars), referrer, country, language and a millisecond
timestamp. That tuple is a reasonable browser fingerprint at event level, and
"aggregate pageviews only" is not what the schema does.

**This is pre-existing, not a regression** — `user_agent` dates to `38c6329`
(2026-08-10) and `site/db/` and `site/worker/` are untouched in this audit
window. Both prior audits missed it. Options: drop `user_agent` (nothing in
`pageviewStats` reads it), or amend the rule in `CLAUDE.md` to describe what
is actually collected. Either is fine; the two disagreeing is not.

The brief was to maximise what each surface holds dearest. Naming the
three moats first made the gaps obvious, and all three were the same

### `/heritage/:id` — 571 permalinks

One page per Fine Arts register entry, computed from
`heritage-register.json`. Each leads with **how the record was located**,
not with the monument's name: an OSM relocation hands over the object id
as a link and names the match quality (`exact` 129, `fuzzy` 15, `prefix`
4, `rank-suffix` 3), so a reader who does not trust the pin can check it.
The Royal Gazette entry is set out as เล่ม / ตอน / วันที่ / เรื่อง — the
form a Thai conservation officer would cite.

The distinctive page is the one with nothing on it. **260 entries render
no map**, and say why: the register's coordinate rounds to about a
kilometre, nothing in OpenStreetMap matched the name, and a pin here
would be a guess dressed as a fact. That page also names the fix —
add the name in OSM and the next build finds it.

Wired through: the explorer panel, the sitemap, and the war room's fire
panel, which now names and links the monument nearest a detection instead
of showing an array index.

### Atlas evidence mode (`E`)

The same massing, recoloured by how each height was established, on one
ordinal ramp — bright where someone recorded a dimension, drab where the
model inferred one. **The city goes mostly drab, and that is the correct
picture**: of 9,415 extruded features, 7 carry a published dimension,
5,987 an OpenStreetMap height tag, 156 a curated value, and **3,265 —
35% — a height nobody ever recorded.**

The engineering that matters is underneath. The ladder lives in
`app/data/evidence-tiers.ts`; the MapLibre expressions are *built* from
it rather than restated, the inspector card reads the same table, and
`build-evidence-tally.mjs` counts the real corpus into
`evidence-tally.json` at build time. **The build fails if the data
contains a height source no tier claims** — otherwise a new rule in a
generator would silently drop its buildings into "inferred" and the atlas
would quietly understate its own evidence.

### `/shophouses/cluster/:slug` — 32 clusters

The join that was missing. Each page states the cluster's screened count
against the citywide signature, the Ministerial Regulation clauses its
own geometry triggers (with the rule text and the count each binds), the
pressure quadrant split with the unclassified remainder *named rather
than dropped*, and the appraised land beneath it — then a section headed
"What this page cannot tell you" listing storeys, tenure, condition,
road width.

The figure worth the page: on Tanao Road, **36 rows exceed today's
10-unit cap**. Those buildings could not lawfully be rebuilt as they
stand, which makes demolition there irreversible whatever replaces it.
That sentence was unavailable before, because the count and the clause
lived in different files.

### Found by looking, again

The selected-language button was invisible on every page of the site.
`.lang-toggle button.is-active` set `background: currentColor` while a
second rule set `color` on the same element — and `currentColor` in a
background resolves against that same element's colour, so the label was
painted on a ground of its own colour. Both halves are named now
(`--lang-on` / `--lang-on-text`), inverted for dark chrome, and pinned by
a test that reads the rule.
